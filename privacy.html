Efaga Privacy Policy

Effective 27 October 2026

Efaga is a food log for iPhone, made by Artem Mazurin, an individual developer based in Cyprus (“I”, “me”). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app.

The short version

Efaga has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Your food log, your profile and your products stay on your iPhone; I never receive them. Two things leave the phone for my server: a recipe you ask Efi to work out, and a short note of which onboarding and subscription screens you reached — see below.

What stays on your phone

  • Your food log — what you ate, portions, times and meals.
  • Your profile — sex, birth year, height, weight, activity level and goal, used only to estimate a daily calorie norm on the device.
  • Your products and meals — anything you add or edit.

This data is stored in a local database inside the app. It is not uploaded to me or to anyone else. Like other app data, it may be included in your device backups if you use iCloud or computer backups; those backups are controlled by Apple and by you.

What leaves your phone

To find foods, Efaga uses Open Food Facts, a free public food database run by the non-profit Open Food Facts association (France).

  • Search: when you search, the text you type is sent to Open Food Facts to get matching products.
  • Barcodes: when you scan a package, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts to look the product up.

These requests carry no name, account or identifier of yours. Like any internet request, they reach Open Food Facts’ servers with your device’s IP address. See the Open Food Facts privacy policy.

Adding products to Open Food Facts. Some versions of Efaga can add a product you typed in after a scan found nothing to Open Food Facts, so the next person who scans it gets it. When this is on, the app tells you under the form before you save. Only the product is sent — barcode, name and nutrition values — never anything about you. Contributions become public under the Open Database License.

Efi's kitchen (recipes)

When you ask Efi to work out a dish, the recipe text you wrote and the names of your own products (names only, no numbers, no log) are sent to my server on Cloudflare and from there to Anthropic, whose AI model reads the recipe. They are used only to answer that one request: my server does not store the recipe or the product names and does not write them to its logs, and Anthropic processes them under its commercial terms, which do not allow training on them.

To keep the daily limit of recipes, the request carries a random ID created by the app when it was installed. It is not linked to your name or email, and my server keeps only a daily count against it, deleted after two days. My server's own log keeps only technical details of each request (the install ID, the result, how long it took, a short reason on failure, and which AI model answered and how many tokens it used). As with any internet request, Cloudflare, which hosts the server, sees your device's IP address and may record it in its request logs. The saved dish and the text you wrote stay on your phone.

Which screens you reached

To learn where people give up before they start, the app tells my server when you open it for the first time, which onboarding step you see, whether you finished or skipped it, when you see the Efi Plus screen, which plan you pick and whether a purchase or a restore went through. Each note carries only the step's name, a few words such as where the screen was opened from, the time, the app version and the same random install ID as Efi's recipes. Never your food, weight, height, age or anything you typed.

As with Efi, Cloudflare sees your IP address when the note arrives; the note itself does not keep it. The notes are kept by Cloudflare Workers Analytics Engine for three months and then deleted. The install ID is random, made by the app and gone if you delete it; it is not tied to your name, email or your phone's hardware (for Efi Plus, see below how the App Store's purchase record is linked to it). The notes are not shared with anyone and are not used for advertising or tracking.

Efi Plus (subscription)

Efi Plus is sold by Apple through the App Store: Apple takes the payment, and I never see your card, name or Apple ID. To know whether a subscription is active, the app uses RevenueCat, a subscription service. RevenueCat receives the same random install ID and the App Store's record of the purchase (which plan, when it started, when it renews or ends, the price and the store country). It does not receive your food, weight or anything you typed, and the app does not let it collect device identifiers for advertising. Before Efi reads a recipe, my server asks RevenueCat whether that install ID has an active subscription and remembers a "yes" for up to a day. I never see your Apple ID: RevenueCat links the install ID to the App Store's purchase record, not me. Like Cloudflare, RevenueCat sees the IP address the request comes from. RevenueCat processes this under its privacy policy.

If you ask for a reminder before the free trial ends, it is a notification scheduled on your phone; nothing is sent anywhere to set it.

Camera

Efaga asks for the camera only to read barcodes. The camera image is processed on the device; no photos are taken, stored or sent.

Reports you share

If you create a PDF report, it is made on your phone and goes only where you send it through the iOS share sheet. It contains your logged food and your daily norm, not your weight, height or age.

Deleting your data

You can delete single entries in the Diary. To delete everything, delete the app from your iPhone — that removes its database for good. I hold no copy, so there is nothing to request from me.

Children

Efaga is not intended for children. The daily norm can only be calculated for people 16 and over.

Health information

The daily norm is an estimate from the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, not medical advice. Talk to a doctor before changing how you eat.
